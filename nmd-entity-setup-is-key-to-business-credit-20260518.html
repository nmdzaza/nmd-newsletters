Entity Setup Is Key To Business Credit

May 18, 2026

When it comes to building business credit, **entity setup is key**. Most coaches will tell you to set up a Delaware LLC, but what they won't tell you is that **Nevada and Wyoming are often better options**. Nevada, for example, has **no state income tax** and a relatively low annual report fee of $350. Wyoming, on the other hand, has **no state income tax** and an annual report fee of just $50. These states offer more benefits for businesses looking to establish credit, making them a more attractive option for entrepreneurs.

To establish business credit, you need to have a **solid entity setup**, including an **EIN from the IRS** and a **business bank account**. Most business owners start with a business checking account from their local bank or credit union, but this can limit their access to credit. Instead, consider opening a business checking account with a bank like **Wells Fargo or Bank of America**, which offer more **business credit options**. These banks have a wider range of financial products and services that can help you build your business credit profile.

When it comes to building business credit, **the key is to start with vendor accounts that report to the business credit bureaus**, such as **Dun & Bradstreet and Experian**. Some of the best vendors to start with include **Uline, Grainger, and Home Depot**, which offer **net 30 terms** and report to the credit bureaus. By following these steps, you can establish a **strong business credit profile** and access more **funding options** for your business. This can include lines of credit, loans, and other financial products that can help you grow your business.

Building business credit takes time and effort, but it's worth it in the long run. With a **strong business credit profile**, you can qualify for better interest rates, higher credit limits, and more favorable terms. You can also use your business credit to **separate your personal and business finances**, which can help you avoid personal liability and protect your personal assets.

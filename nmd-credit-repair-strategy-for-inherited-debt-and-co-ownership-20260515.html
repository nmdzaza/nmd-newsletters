Credit Repair Strategy For Inherited Debt And Co-Ownership

May 15, 2026

Inherited debt and co-ownership issues can be a significant obstacle for those trying to build or repair their credit. A 22-year-old trying to save their family home from debt is just one example of the many individuals struggling with these problems. To tackle this challenge, it's essential to focus on **disputing inaccurate or incomplete information** on your credit reports, particularly in the Metro2 fields. These fields contain critical information about your accounts, and any errors can have a significant impact on your credit score.

When reviewing your credit report, **look for errors in fields like "Account Status" (Field 26) or "Account Type" (Field 24)**. These fields can contain incorrect information that can be disputed under **FCRA Section 609**, which requires furnishers to conduct a reasonable investigation. If you find any errors, you can dispute them by writing a letter to the credit bureau, citing the specific Metro2 field and the incorrect information. For example, "I am disputing the account status listed as 'charged off' in Field 26, as I have documentation showing the account was paid in full." Be sure to **include your account information, the specific error, and a clear request for correction**.

When disputing these errors, you can also **reference FDCPA Section 809**, which prohibits furnishers from reporting inaccurate information. Additionally, **request that the credit bureau verify the debt under FCRA Section 609**, which requires the furnisher to provide proof of the debt. If they cannot provide this proof, **the debt must be removed from your report**. This is especially important for those dealing with inherited debt or co-ownership issues, as it can help you avoid unnecessary damage to your credit score.

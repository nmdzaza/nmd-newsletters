Millionaires And Billionaires Pay Minimal Taxes By Utilizing Entity Structuring

April 05, 2026

The wealthy have long understood the importance of minimizing tax liability through strategic entity structuring and write-offs. By creating separate companies in different regions, millionaires and billionaires are able to significantly reduce their tax burden. For example, **forming an LLC in Delaware** can help avoid state taxes on intangible income, such as royalties and interest. Meanwhile, **establishing a C-Corp in Nevada** can provide an additional layer of tax protection. By taking advantage of these strategies, business owners can keep more of their hard-earned money and invest it back into their companies.

Another key strategy employed by the wealthy is the use of **shelf corporations**, which are pre-formed entities that have already established credit profiles. This allows business owners to bypass the typical 6-12 month waiting period for credit establishment, instantly accessing higher credit limits and better interest rates. Companies like Dun & Bradstreet and Experian require a minimum of $1,000 to $5,000 in reported payments to establish a credible credit profile. By acquiring a shelf corp, business owners can qualify for **$50,000 to $100,000 credit lines** with vendors like Nav or CreditBuilderPlus.

To replicate this strategy, entrepreneurs and small business owners should focus on **building a strong business credit profile** by reporting payments to the major credit bureaus. This process begins with obtaining an EIN from the IRS and registering your business with the state. Next, apply for a **business credit card with a low credit limit**, such as the Wells Fargo Business Secured Credit Card, and make regular payments to establish a positive credit history. As your credit profile grows, you can apply for **higher credit limits and more favorable terms** with vendors like Office Depot or Staples.

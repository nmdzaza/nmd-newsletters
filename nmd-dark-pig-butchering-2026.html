He Built a $73M Pig-Butchering Empire.
Then Cut His Ankle Monitor and Ran.

Daren Li ran one of the largest crypto romance scams in U.S. history — dating apps, fake trading platforms, shell companies, Cambodian fraud compounds. He fled mid-case. A federal judge sentenced him to 20 years anyway.

📅 April 5, 2026 ✍️ NMD ZAZA ⏰ 8 min read

The con starts with a DM. It ends with everything you have.

It's a Tuesday night. You're on a dating app or scrolling social media when someone messages you — friendly, professional, attractive profile. They're not asking for anything weird. Just conversation. They seem sharp. Educated. Maybe they mention they've been doing well in crypto investments lately. They'd love to share what they know.

That's the entry point for what law enforcement calls "pig butchering" — and it's the most effective financial crime operating in the world right now. The scammer fattens the pig before the slaughter. Weeks of relationship-building. Small investment wins to build confidence. Then a big push — and by the time you realize what's happened, your money is gone, converted to crypto, and sitting in an offshore account operated out of a Southeast Asian fraud compound you've never heard of.

This is exactly how Daren Li built a $73.6 million criminal operation — and why a federal judge just dropped 20 years on him, even while he was somewhere on the run.

Who is Daren Li — and how big was this thing?

Daren Li, a dual national of China and St. Kitts and Nevis, wasn't running some basement operation. He was one of the primary money laundering architects behind an international cryptocurrency investment fraud network operating out of the Kingdom of Cambodia. His job in the conspiracy: make sure the stolen American money disappeared cleanly.

Here's how the machine worked. Unindicted co-conspirators — operators inside Cambodian fraud compounds — would target Americans through dating apps, social media, and unsolicited texts. They'd build romantic or professional connections over weeks, sometimes months. Then they'd introduce victims to what looked like legitimate crypto trading platforms. Spoofed domains, professional interfaces, fake account dashboards showing real gains.

Victims would deposit. See their "portfolio" grow. Reinvest. Bring in family members. And then one day — either they try to withdraw and hit invented fees, or the platform just vanishes.

How the Money Moved

Victim funds were deposited directly into bank accounts controlled by Li and his co-conspirators. At least $59.8 million flowed through U.S. shell companies designed specifically to launder the proceeds. The cash was then converted to cryptocurrency and moved offshore. By the time victims realized what happened, the trail was buried in layers of shell entities.

Li's role was to receive the victim funds and ensure the laundering process worked. And it did — until federal investigators started mapping the financial network. At least $73.6 million in victim funds were confirmed in Li's plea agreement. Eight co-conspirators have already pleaded guilty. Li was the first major recipient of victim funds to face sentencing.

He pleaded guilty — then ran.

Here's where the story gets cinematic. Li was arrested, charged, and pleaded guilty to money-laundering conspiracy. He was fitted with an electronic ankle monitoring device and released pending sentencing. Standard procedure for defendants cooperating with federal proceedings.

In December 2025, Daren Li cut off the ankle monitor and disappeared.

The federal court in California didn't blink. On February 10, 2026, Judge sentenced Li in absentia — meaning Li wasn't even in the room when the gavel came down. The judge imposed the statutory maximum: 20 years in federal prison plus 3 years of supervised release.

"They generate continuous cash flow, target victims globally, and rely on repeatable social-engineering models."

That's Ari Redbord of TRM Labs, a blockchain intelligence firm, describing why pig-butchering networks are so dangerous — and why they keep scaling even as individual operators get caught. The model is a franchise. Daren Li was one node in a network that spans Cambodia, Myanmar, and elsewhere in Southeast Asia, where fraud compounds operate with near impunity, often using trafficked labor to run the actual scam outreach.

What "pig butchering" actually means — and why it works

The term comes from the Chinese phrase "sha zhu pan." The victim is the pig. The scammer fattens them first — investing time, emotional energy, fake wins — before the final slaughter. It's calculated. Clinical. And devastatingly effective against people who think they're too smart to get scammed.

Phase Method Duration
Phase 1 — Grooming DMs via dating apps, social media, random texts 2–8 weeks
Phase 2 — Trust Build Fake professional relationship, crypto "mentor" role 1–4 weeks
Phase 3 — Small Wins Victim deposits small amount, sees fake gains, withdraws successfully 1–2 weeks
Phase 4 — The Push Major investment encouraged, often with family funds or borrowed money Days
Phase 5 — The Slaughter Platform locks up, fake "taxes" demanded, or disappears entirely Immediate

The reason this hits credit files isn't just the money lost. It's what happens after. Victims drain savings, take personal loans, even second-mortgage their homes to fund what they believe is a legitimate investment. When the scam completes, they're sitting on debt — credit cards maxed, HELOC balance due, personal loan payments they can't make — while their "portfolio" is worth zero.

Credit Damage Pathway

Pig-butchering victims often experience a cascade: savings depleted, debt obligations remain, missed payments begin within 30–60 days, score drops 100+ points. Because the scam isn't traditional identity theft, many victims don't even know which FCRA provisions protect them — and bureaus don't flag it automatically. You have to know how to fight it.

What You Should Know

The scale is bigger than any one case. The DOJ's Operation Fortune Sweep and related takedowns have identified hundreds of millions in pig-butchering losses from U.S. victims alone. The $73.6 million tied to Li is one confirmed slice of a much larger operation.

The operators are often trafficking victims themselves. Many of the people running the actual scam scripts inside Cambodian and Myanmar compounds were trafficked there under false promises of legitimate work. The fraud network is layered — financial crime and human trafficking in the same operation.

Li is still out there. As of sentencing in February 2026, Daren Li is a federal fugitive. He has a 20-year sentence waiting for him if caught. His co-conspirators who stayed and cooperated are working through their own plea agreements.

If you've been targeted or victimized, report immediately. File with the FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center at ic3.gov and the FTC at reportfraud.ftc.gov. If you lost money and subsequently missed payments or took on debt, document everything — the FCRA has provisions for financial harm caused by fraud that can support bureau disputes, but you need the paper trail.

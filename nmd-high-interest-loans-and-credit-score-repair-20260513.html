High-Interest Loans And Credit Score Repair

May 13, 2026

High-interest loans can be a significant burden for anyone, especially when they're accompanied by poor credit scores. Recent examples, such as the 18% interest $35k car loan, highlight the need for **strategic credit score repair**. If you're struggling with a high-interest loan, the first step is to check your credit report for any **inaccuracies or incomplete information** that may be affecting your credit score. According to **FCRA Section 609**, you have the right to dispute any information on your credit report that is inaccurate or incomplete.

When reviewing your credit report, pay close attention to the **Metro2 fields**, particularly the "**Account Status**" and "**Payment History**" fields. These fields can have a significant impact on your credit score, and any errors can be disputed. If you find any errors, write a **dispute letter** to the credit bureau citing **FCRA Section 611**, which requires the bureau to investigate and verify the disputed information. In your dispute letter, specifically state what you are disputing and why, and request that the credit bureau **delete or correct the inaccurate information**. For example, you can write: "I am disputing the account status listed on my credit report, which shows a late payment in January. However, I have documentation showing that I made the payment on time. I request that you investigate and verify this information, and delete or correct the inaccurate information pursuant to **FCRA Section 611**."

In addition to disputing errors on your credit report, consider **strategies to pay off high-interest loans quickly**. Some people, like the 22-year-old with the $35k car loan, may benefit from **snowballing or consolidating their debt**. However, before making any major financial decisions, it's essential to review your loan's **amortization table**. This will help you understand how much of each payment is going towards **interest and principal**, and make informed decisions about your debt repayment strategy. By taking control of your debt and credit score, you can save money and build a stronger financial foundation.

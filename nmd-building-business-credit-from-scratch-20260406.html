Building Business Credit From Scratch

April 06, 2026

Building business credit from scratch is a crucial step for entrepreneurs looking to establish a strong financial foundation for their company. However, most entrepreneurs think that building business credit is all about having a good personal credit score, but that's not entirely true. **The right entity setup is essential** to build business credit from scratch. Forming an LLC in a business-friendly state like Wyoming or Delaware is highly recommended, where filing fees are low - $100 in Wyoming and $90 in Delaware.

Once you have your LLC, you need to get an EIN from the IRS, which is free, and then open a business bank account with a bank like Bank of America or Wells Fargo. **Getting an EIN and opening a business bank account are critical steps** in establishing your business's financial identity. Now, here's where most business credit coaches get it wrong - they tell you to apply for credit with vendors like Home Depot or Lowe's, but the truth is, these vendors have strict approval requirements and often require a personal guarantee.

Instead, **start with vendors like Quill or Uline**, which offer net-30 terms and don't require a personal guarantee. These vendors are more accessible and can help you establish a positive payment history. To take it to the next level, you need to **monitor your business credit report with the major credit bureaus** - Dun & Bradstreet, Experian, and Equifax. You can get a free report from Nav, but be aware that it's not always accurate. The key is to find vendors that report to these bureaus, like Grainger or Staples, and make timely payments to build your credit score.

For example, **a $1,000 net-30 account with Quill can help you establish a positive payment history**, which can then be used to qualify for larger credit lines.

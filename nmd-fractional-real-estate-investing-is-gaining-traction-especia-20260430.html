The conversation around DSTs is also noteworthy. These trusts allow investors to own a **fraction of a property**, which can provide a **steady income stream** and potentially **lower risk**. Some investors are looking at DSTs as an alternative to traditional **1031 exchanges**, which can be complex and time-consuming. With a DST, investors can own a **percentage of a property**, typically ranging from 1% to 10%, and receive a corresponding percentage of the rental income. This can be an attractive option for those who want to **diversify their portfolio** without committing to a large upfront investment.

The numbers can be compelling. For instance, a $1 million property can be divided into 10 DST units, each representing a 10% ownership stake. If the property generates $50,000 in annual rental income, each DST unit would receive $5,000 in income. This can provide a relatively **stable source of passive income**, with the potential for **long-term appreciation in property value**. But what's the key consideration when evaluating a DST investment? Is it the **property's location**, the **trust's management structure**, or something else entirely?

When evaluating a DST investment, it's essential to consider **multiple factors**, including the property's location, the trust's management structure, and the potential for long-term appreciation in property value. **Due diligence** is crucial to ensure that you're making an informed investment decision. Additionally, you should consider your **investment goals** and **risk tolerance** to determine if a DST investment is right for you.

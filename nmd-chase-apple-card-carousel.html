▼ SLIDE 2 — THE DEAL EXPLAINED ▼
What Just Happened
NMD

JPMorgan Chase
Takes the Apple
Card.

JPMorgan Chase — the biggest bank in the US — has reached a deal to take over the Apple Card program from Goldman Sachs. The program has roughly $20 billion in outstanding balances and millions of cardholders.

Goldman Sachs built the Apple Card partnership in 2019 as a cornerstone of its push into consumer banking. That strategy failed. Goldman is now selling those balances at a $1 billion+ discount just to get out. Chase steps in and becomes the new issuer for both new and existing customers.

Credit Impact

What This
Means for
Your Score.

01

Account Age Can Be Affected

If the account is re-issued as a brand-new account under Chase, your credit history on that card could reset. Account age is 15% of your FICO score — losing years of history hurts.

⚠️
02

New Hard Inquiry Possible

Depending on how Chase structures the transition, some customers may face a new hard inquiry. Hard pulls drop your score 5–10 points temporarily.

🔍
03

Credit Limit Could Change

Chase has its own underwriting standards. Your limit may be adjusted — up or down. A lower limit raises your utilization ratio, which impacts your score directly.

💳
04

Monitor for Reporting Errors

Transfers like this are a known source of credit reporting mistakes. The wrong balance, wrong status, or duplicate account could appear on your report — and most people never check.

What to Watch For

Check your credit report immediately after the transition — errors during issuer changes are common and disputable under the FCRA.

Watch your credit limit. If Chase lowers it, your utilization spikes and your score drops. Know this before it surprises you.

Don't close the card. Even during a transition, closing a card kills account age and raises utilization. Hold it open.

Any reporting error is disputable. Wrong balance, duplicate account, wrong status — each one is leverage under your rights.

Where People Go Wrong

Ignoring the transition entirely. Out of sight, out of mind — until the damage shows up months later on a loan application.

Closing the account during transition. Feels like cleaning house — actually costs you history and raises your utilization ratio.

Not disputing reporting errors. Most people assume the banks get it right. They often don't — and errors linger for years unchallenged.

Applying for replacement cards immediately. Multiple hard inquiries stack up and drag your score before you've had time to monitor the transition.

July 04, 2026

When it comes to disputing errors on your credit report, it's essential to know your rights and the laws that protect you. The Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA) is a federal law that regulates the collection, use, and disclosure of consumer credit information. **§ 609(a) of the FCRA** requires furnishers to conduct a reasonable investigation, including verifying the debt, account status, and payment history, whenever a dispute is filed. This means that if you find an error on your credit report, you can dispute it and the furnisher must investigate and verify the information.

To effectively dispute errors on your credit report, you need to understand the process and the relevant laws. **§ 611(a)(1)(A) of the FCRA** states that if a disputed item is found to be unverifiable, it must be deleted from your credit report. When submitting a dispute to the credit bureau, make sure to cite this section of the law and request that the item be deleted, rather than just updated. This is crucial because if the credit bureau returns an "updated" response instead of a "delete" response, it could lead to **FDCPA § 807(8) violations**, which can have serious consequences.

To pinpoint inaccuracies in your credit report, it's helpful to use the **Metro2 format**, which is a standardized format for reporting credit information. This format allows you to identify and challenge specific data fields, such as **DPV (date of first delinquency)** or **OC (original creditor)**. By examining these fields, you can determine if the information is accurate and complete. When submitting your dispute, be sure to explicitly state the reason for the dispute and the action you're requesting. For example, "I dispute the [account/information] due to [inaccuracy/incompleteness], as required by **§ 611(a)(1)(A)**, and request its immediate deletion."

The Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA) is a powerful tool for consumers, but it's only effective if you know how to use it. **Data furnishers are required by law to conduct a reasonable investigation of disputed information**, but they often don't. That's where you come in - by disputing inaccurate information on your credit report, you can take control of your credit score and force furnishers to prove their claims. The key is to target the right information and follow the right procedures.

When disputing information on your credit report, **target the Metro2 fields**, specifically the 'account status' and 'payment history' fields. These are often the easiest to challenge, and by focusing on these areas, you can maximize your chances of success. Send a dispute letter citing **15 U.S.C. § 1681i**, which requires data furnishers to conduct a reasonable investigation of disputed information. In your letter, **request verification of the debt**, including the original contract and payment records. This will force the furnisher to provide evidence to support their claims, and if they can't, you may be able to have the information removed from your report.

If the furnisher fails to respond or provides inadequate verification, **you can escalate to a complaint with the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB)**, citing **15 U.S.C. § 1691e**. This will trigger an investigation and may result in the furnisher being required to correct or remove the inaccurate information.

Pay-For-Delete Negotiation Scripts

April 04, 2026

When dealing with debt collectors, it's essential to know your rights and how to negotiate effectively. If you're trying to remove a negative mark from your credit report, you may be considering a pay-for-delete agreement. **This type of agreement requires the collector to delete the tradeline from your credit report in exchange for payment**. However, it's crucial to get the agreement in writing to ensure the collector follows through on their obligation.

The script for a pay-for-delete agreement should include **exact language** that outlines the terms of the deal. This language should include: "In exchange for payment of [amount], you agree to delete the tradeline associated with [account number] from all three major credit bureaus, and update the Metro2 field 53 (Account Status) to reflect a deletion." **This ensures the collector understands their obligation to update your credit report**. If the collector refuses to agree to a pay-for-delete, it's time to review your credit report for any inaccuracies that can be disputed.

When reviewing your credit report, pay close attention to the Metro2 fields, particularly **field 24 (Date of First Delinquency)** and **field 26 (Date of Last Activity)**. If these dates are incorrect, you can dispute the tradeline with the credit bureau, citing **FCRA section 623(a)(2)**, which requires furnishers to conduct a reasonable investigation when a consumer disputes the accuracy of information. **Disputing inaccuracies on your credit report can help improve your credit score** and remove negative marks that are dragging you down.

When drafting a dispute letter, be sure to **include specific Metro2 field numbers and the correct information**. For example: "I dispute the tradeline associated with [account number] due to an incorrect Date of First Delinquency (Metro2 field 24) which is currently listed as [incorrect date]. The correct date is [correct date]. I request that you update this field and conduct a reasonable investigation pursuant to FCRA section 623(a)(2)." **Being precise and detailed in your dispute letter is key to a successful dispute**.

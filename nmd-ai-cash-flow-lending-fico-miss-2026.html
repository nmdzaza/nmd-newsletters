The FICO Problem Nobody Talks About

FICO's model was built in 1989. It was designed to evaluate people who were already embedded in the credit system — people with credit cards, installment loans, and documented repayment histories stretching back years. If you have all of that, great. FICO works for you.

But roughly 25 million Americans lack sufficient recent credit activity to generate a usable score. These aren't deadbeats. Many of them are immigrants who built lives outside U.S. credit infrastructure. Gig workers whose income doesn't flow through traditional accounts. Young adults who never opened a credit card because nobody taught them why they should. People who paid off everything and let their accounts go dormant.

FICO doesn't see them. It scores what it knows, and it knows nothing about your rent, your utilities, your DoorDash driver income, or whether you've paid your phone bill on time for four years straight. So it labels them invisible — and invisible people don't get loans.

"Traditional credit scoring models fail to capture financial behavior that occurs outside conventional credit products. The gap isn't risk — it's the model."

That gap — between a person's actual financial behavior and what FICO can see — is exactly where AI underwriting is stepping in.

How Cash-Flow Underwriting Actually Works

Cash-flow-based underwriting does something that sounds obvious once you hear it: it looks at your bank account. Income deposits. Expense patterns. Whether you consistently cover your rent. Whether your recurring obligations — utilities, subscriptions, phone bill — get paid on time every month. Whether you have a cushion or you're running to zero every two weeks.

This is the financial picture that banks have always had access to but never used for underwriting decisions. AI changes that. With open banking data and machine-learning models trained on millions of transactions, lenders can now evaluate your creditworthiness in real time based on how you actually manage money — not on whether you opened a Discover card in college.

The Shift in Plain English

Old underwriting asked: "Do you have a credit history?" New AI underwriting asks: "Do you manage money responsibly?" Those are very different questions — and a lot of people who fail the first one pass the second.

The models don't just look at a snapshot. They analyze consistency, volatility, and resilience over time. Is your income stable or erratic? Do you recover quickly after a financial shock, or do you spiral? How much of your income goes to fixed obligations versus discretionary spending? What's your runway if income stopped for 30 days?

This is not a FICO score with extra steps. It's a fundamentally different evaluation of financial health — and it's more accurate for a huge segment of the population.

Chase and PayPal Are Already In

This isn't some startup moonshot. The largest institutions in consumer finance are already deploying cash-flow underwriting at scale. JPMorgan Chase has integrated cash-flow data partnerships to power underwriting capabilities for borrowers who wouldn't qualify under traditional models. PayPal has done the same, using transaction-level behavioral data from its own platform to extend credit to merchants and consumers that traditional lenders couldn't evaluate.

Both have partnered with data providers like Nova Credit to access alternative financial histories — including credit files from other countries, which matters enormously for the immigrant population that traditional FICO data has always excluded.

The regulatory environment is also shifting to support this. The Office of the Comptroller of the Currency has publicly recognized the value of alternative data in credit decisions when used responsibly. The CFPB's open banking rule — requiring financial institutions to share data with consumer permission — makes this even more accessible. The infrastructure for cash-flow underwriting is being built into the system at every level.

What This Means If You Have a Thin File or a Bad Score

Here's the move that most people miss: this shift doesn't replace the need to fix your credit — it expands your options while you do it. If your FICO score is damaged, cash-flow underwriting gives you access to lenders who will evaluate your current behavior instead of your past mistakes. That's a bridge.

But if your cash flow doesn't look good either — if you're overdrafting, if your income is inconsistent, if your expenses are out of control — no AI model is going to save you. The same principles apply. The model just changed.

Don't Sleep on This

AI underwriting can see your positive behavior that FICO misses — but it can also see your negative behavior more clearly than ever. Consistent overdrafts, irregular income, and erratic spending all show up in cash-flow data. Cleaning your credit AND cleaning your money habits is the double play.

The practical upside is real, though. If you've been paying rent on time for years — through a landlord, through a rent payment service, or reported to the bureaus via programs like Experian Boost — that behavior is now increasingly visible to the lenders that matter. If you run your finances through a bank account with clean, consistent activity, you have a case to make to AI underwriters that you never had with FICO.

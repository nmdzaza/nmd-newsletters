Fractional real estate investing is gaining traction as a viable means to diversify investment portfolios without the need for a substantial upfront investment of $100K. As interest rates continue to climb, many investors are left wondering why property prices remain high. In regions like Colorado, **house hacking with Accessory Dwelling Units (ADUs) is not always a feasible option** due to exorbitant build costs and relatively low rental income. For instance, a $300k build with a rental income of $1,600/month would take over **15 years to pay back**, excluding other expenses.

The issue of pricing also affects those seeking to invest in turn-key properties. While purchasing a turn-key duplex for $165k in cash off-market may seem like a good deal, **the lack of transparency regarding improvements made** makes it challenging to assess the property's value accurately. This is where **fractional investing** comes into play, allowing multiple investors to own a portion of a property and significantly lowering the barrier to entry. For example, with platforms like Lofty's fractional real estate marketplace, investors can invest in properties with **much lower upfront costs**.

In today's market, it's crucial to carefully evaluate key metrics when making investment decisions. One essential metric is the **vacancy rate**, as a high vacancy rate can significantly impact cash flow. For instance, if a property has a **10% vacancy rate** and the monthly rent is $1,600, that's $160 per month in lost income, which translates to **$1,920 per year**. Considering these factors is vital when evaluating potential investments. Other critical metrics include **exit cap**, **rehab costs**, and **cash flow**, among others.

When assessing potential investments, it's essential to have a clear understanding of the key metrics and how they impact the investment's viability. **A thorough analysis of the property's financials**, including income statements and expense reports, is crucial. Additionally, **researching the local market trends** and **understanding the property's potential for appreciation** can help investors make informed decisions. By carefully evaluating these factors, investors can minimize risks and maximize returns.

The age-old debate among real estate investors is whether to hold onto rental properties or sell them. A recent discussion that caught our attention involved an investor who's been holding onto a property for less than a year, but the tenants have been living there for 10 years and are paying significantly below market rent. The investor is torn between selling and allowing the tenants to stay on a month-to-month basis, highlighting the challenges of being a landlord and the importance of having a **solid investment strategy**.

Another interesting scenario involves an investor building a new house and deciding whether to sell their current property or hold onto it as a rental. With an expected monthly income of $17,000, they're weighing the pros and cons of using that income to pay off their mortgage or invest in more properties. This situation raises crucial questions about **cash flow**, **debt servicing**, and **investment goals**. For instance, holding onto the property could generate an annual return of 8-10% through rental income, but the investor would also need to consider the costs of **maintenance**, **property management**, and potential **vacancies**.

The Lofty marketplace is also relevant to these discussions, as it provides a platform for **fractional real estate investing**. By tokenizing properties, investors can buy and sell fractions of a property, allowing for more **flexibility** and **liquidity**. This could be a game-changer for investors who don't have a lot of capital but still want to get into the real estate market. For example, if an investor wants to buy a $100,000 property, they could buy 10% of the property for $10,000, rather than having to come up with the full $100,000.

So, what's the most important factor to consider when deciding whether to hold onto a rental property or sell it? Is it the **potential for long-term appreciation**, the **current cash flow**, or something else entirely? The answer will vary depending on individual circumstances and investment goals.

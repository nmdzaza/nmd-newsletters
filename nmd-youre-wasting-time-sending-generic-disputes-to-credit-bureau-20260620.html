June 20, 2026

When it comes to disputing errors on your credit report, **specificity is key**. Under 15 U.S.C. § 1681i(a)(1)(A) of the FCRA, you need to clearly state the reason for your dispute, such as "**account not mine**" or "**date of first delinquency is incorrect**". This is not a suggestion, it's a requirement. By being specific, you're forcing the credit bureau to take your dispute seriously and conduct a thorough investigation.

When drafting your dispute, it's essential to reference **16 CFR § 660.2**, which emphasizes the furnisher's obligation to conduct a **reasonable investigation**. You should demand that the bureau verify the **ECOA/FCRA/FTC compliance** of the furnisher's investigation procedure, citing **15 U.S.C. § 1691e(a)**. This ensures that the furnisher is held accountable for their actions and that your dispute is handled fairly.

If the credit bureau doesn't comply with your request, **don't be afraid to take it to the next level**. You can threaten to file a complaint with the **CFPB**, referencing **12 U.S.C. § 5536(a)(2)**. This shows that you're serious about resolving the issue and willing to take action to protect your rights. To ensure you have proof of mailing, it's crucial to send your disputes via **certified mail**, as per **15 U.S.C. § 1681i(a)(3)**.

These fields contain vital information about your credit accounts, and errors can significantly impact your credit score. By leveraging these errors in your dispute, you can **strengthen your case** and increase the chances of a successful resolution.

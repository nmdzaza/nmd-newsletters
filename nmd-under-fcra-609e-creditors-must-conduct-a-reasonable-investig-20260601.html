Under Fcra § 609(E), Creditors Must Conduct A Reasonable Investigation Of Disput

June 01, 2026

When it comes to disputing errors on your credit report, **knowing your rights is key**. Under the Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA), creditors are required to conduct a reasonable investigation of disputes. However, they often fail to meet this standard, especially for old or complex accounts. This is where you can take matters into your own hands and demand a more thorough review.

To maximize your chances of success, **focus on the Metro2 fields that creditors are most likely to get wrong**. The Date of First Delinquency and the Current Balance are two areas where errors frequently occur. By targeting these specific fields, you can increase the likelihood of finding errors and having them corrected. Additionally, **demand that the creditor provide a complete account history**, including all payments and credits, as required by the Fair Debt Collection Practices Act (FDCPA) § 809(a). This will give you a clear picture of your account and help you identify any discrepancies.

One effective tactic to use when disputing errors is the **"reinvestigation demand"**. After receiving the initial dispute response, send a follow-up letter stating that the creditor's investigation was inadequate and demanding a second, more thorough review. This can lead to deletions or significant score improvements, as the creditor is forced to re-examine the account and correct any errors. By using this tactic, you can **take control of the dispute process and ensure that your credit report is accurate**.

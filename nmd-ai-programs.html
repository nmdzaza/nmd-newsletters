Every AI Program We Run — And Why We Built Them

April 18, 2026

AI Literacy Curriculum — Ages 10 to 17

Most schools are still teaching kids how to use apps. We're teaching them how to think with AI.

Our curriculum is built around one principle: if a kid can't explain the output, they haven't learned anything. Every session ends with a deliverable — a project, a pitch, a product. Not a quiz.

We run two tracks. Ages 10–13 use AI as a creative tool — storytelling, design, problem-solving. Ages 14–17 treat it as a career and business skill. Same tools, different stakes.

What we don't do: certification badges with no project work behind them. One-size-fits-all curricula. Hour-long "AI awareness" sessions that teach nothing.

AI Workshops in Underserved Communities

Our first community workshop didn't survive week one. We had slides, definitions, a traditional setup. Half the room checked out by day two.

So we scrapped it and rebuilt from the ground up. Open with a problem they already have. "You need a job. Here's how AI writes a resume that gets past the filter." Now they're engaged.

We also built the program to run on a phone, a library computer, or a borrowed tablet — because reliable personal devices aren't a given in the communities we serve.

By week three, participants are teaching each other. That's how you know it stuck.

Partnering with Miami to Build an AI Workforce Pipeline

Miami has the infrastructure. What it's been missing is a private partner that can actually deliver AI training at a community level — not a 2-hour awareness session, but a real program with real outcomes.

That's the gap NMD Solutions is filling.

The city brings facilities, outreach, and funding pipelines. We bring the curriculum, the instructors, the tech stack, and the accountability layer. We track what participants actually learn — not just attendance.

The goal is a self-sustaining pipeline. Youth who complete the program have a direct path to becoming the next cohort of instructors. Government moves slow. We planned for that. We showed up with results instead of a pitch deck.

AI for People With Zero Tech Background

The biggest lie in tech education: "no experience necessary." Most courses that say that still assume you've used a search engine strategically. We built for people who genuinely have no foundation.

We call our framework Context First, Tool Second.

Before anyone touches an AI tool, they answer one question: what problem am I solving right now? We run AI exercises on paper first. "What would you say to a really smart assistant who could do anything you asked? Write it down." That's your first prompt. Now let's type it.

Every session ends with something the participant can use today — a cover letter, a business idea outline, a customer response. Not a quiz. A deliverable.

ScoreBoost — Credit Repair Plus the Education Layer

ScoreBoost started as a credit repair tool. Telegram bot, dispute letters, AI-powered — built for people who couldn't afford a $500/month credit repair service. It worked.

But we kept running into the same wall. We'd fix someone's credit. Six months later, same negative patterns. Not because the disputes didn't work — because nothing changed about how they understood credit in the first place.

The education layer isn't an add-on. It covers how scoring models actually work, why disputing alone isn't a strategy, and how to use credit as a leverage tool — not just a number to manage.

The credit repair industry makes money on repeat clients. We're building to eliminate repeat clients. ScoreBoost gives you the score. The education layer gives you the knowledge to keep it.

AI Business Skills for Adults Who Missed the First Wave

If you weren't in tech in 2015, it felt like the door closed. Same feeling for social media, crypto, e-commerce. AI is moving faster than any of those waves — and the message most adults keep getting is: you're already behind.

We built a course that rejects that premise entirely.

Eight weeks. No fluff. Week 1–2: AI as an employee — how to delegate, give instructions, quality-check results. Week 3–4: AI for income — content, client communication, lead generation. Week 5–6: AI for operations — automations, scheduling, document management. Week 7–8: AI strategy — how to stay current and build your personal AI stack.

You didn't miss the wave. The wave is still building.

A key strategy to improve credit scores is to focus on the **Metro2 fields that directly impact the credit calculation**, particularly the "Payment History" and "Credit Utilization" fields. **Understanding these fields is crucial**, as they can make or break your credit score. According to **FCRA Section 609**, consumers have the right to dispute inaccurate or incomplete information on their credit reports. When writing a dispute letter, **specify the exact Metro2 field that is being disputed**, such as "Payment History - Late Payment" or "Credit Utilization - High Balance".

For example, if a credit report shows a late payment that was actually made on time, the dispute letter should state: "I am disputing the late payment reported on [date] for [account name], as I have proof of payment on [date of payment]". **Being specific and providing proof is key** to a successful dispute. It's also crucial to understand the **investigation procedures that credit bureaus must follow**, as outlined in **FCRA Section 611**. Credit bureaus have 30 days to investigate disputes and must provide consumers with the results of the investigation.

If the dispute is upheld, the credit bureau must **correct the error and provide a revised credit report**. Furnishers of information, such as banks and credit card companies, also have obligations under the FCRA, including the duty to **investigate disputes and correct errors**, as stated in **FCRA Section 623**. To take control of your credit, **review your report carefully** and look for any errors or inaccuracies in the Metro2 fields. If you find any, **write a clear and specific dispute letter**, citing the relevant FCRA sections and Metro2 fields.

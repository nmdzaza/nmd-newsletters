If You'Re Disputing Errors On Your Credit Report, You Need To Know That Furnishe

June 14, 2026

When it comes to disputing errors on your credit report, time is of the essence. Under the Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA), **furnishers have 30 days to investigate and respond** to your dispute. However, it's crucial that you provide a **specific dispute reason**, such as "account not mine" or "paid in full", to trigger a thorough investigation. Generic disputes can lead to generic responses, and you may not get the resolution you're looking for. To avoid this, use the **Metro2 format** to specify the exact data field in error, giving the furnisher clear guidance on what needs to be corrected.

Using the Metro2 format is key to getting results. By pinpointing the exact error, you're forcing the furnisher to **investigate and respond** to your specific claim. This can help prevent unnecessary back-and-forth and ensure that your dispute is handled efficiently. Additionally, if the furnisher fails to respond or fix the error, you can **sue for damages** under the FCRA and the Fair Debt Collection Practices Act (FDCPA). **15 U.S.C. § 1681n and 15 U.S.C. § 1692k** are the specific sections that give you the right to take action against furnishers who fail to comply with the law.

To take it to the next level, consider using a **604 dispute letter**, which cites the specific FCRA and FDCPA sections that apply to your case. This sends a strong message to the furnisher that you're serious about getting your credit report corrected. And if you're still not getting the results you want, **file a complaint with the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB)**. The CFPB is a powerful ally in the fight against credit reporting errors, and they can help you get the resolution you deserve.

So, what's the best way to get started? **Don't waste time with generic disputes** or vague claims. Instead, use the Metro2 format to specify the exact error, and follow up with a 604 dispute letter and a complaint to the CFPB if necessary.

April 07, 2026

The concept of **cash for keys** has been gaining traction in the real estate investing world, with some investors considering offering tenants a lump sum to vacate the premises in order to close a deal. This approach can be beneficial in certain situations, as it allows the buyer to take possession of the property quickly and avoid the hassle of dealing with existing tenants. However, it's essential to **weigh the costs and benefits** of this approach, as offering cash for keys can be a significant expense.

For example, if an investor has 40 units and needs to offer cash for keys to 10 tenants, the cost could be substantial. Let's assume the investor offers **$2,000 per tenant**, which is a reasonable estimate. This would translate to a total cost of **$20,000**. However, if the investor is looking to sell the property for **$1 million**, the cost of cash for keys would only be **2% of the total sale price**. In this scenario, offering cash for keys might be a worthwhile investment to ensure a smooth transaction. On the other hand, if the investor is looking to hold onto the property and generate rental income, the cost of cash for keys might be too high.

Another factor to consider is the potential for **renovation costs**. In one instance, an investor is considering purchasing a 4plex for **$330,000**, which works out to **$82,500 per door**. However, the renovation models don't pencil out, which could be a major obstacle. If the investor needs to spend **$20,000 to $50,000 per unit** on renovations, the total cost could be substantial. In this scenario, the investor might need to reconsider the purchase price or explore alternative financing options. **Careful planning and financial analysis** are crucial to determining whether cash for keys is a viable strategy for a particular investment opportunity.

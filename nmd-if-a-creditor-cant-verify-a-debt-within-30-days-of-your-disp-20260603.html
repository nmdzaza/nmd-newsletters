June 03, 2026

When dealing with debt collectors, it's essential to know your rights and how to assert them. If a creditor is unable to verify a debt within **30 days of your dispute**, they are required by law to **delete it**. This is stated in **15 U.S.C. § 1681i(a)(6)**, a section of the Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA) that protects consumers from inaccurate or unverifiable information on their credit reports. To initiate this process, you'll need to send a **certified letter** to the creditor, clearly stating your dispute and citing **15 U.S.C. § 1692g**.

In your letter, be sure to **demand specific documentation**, including the original contract, payment history, and proof of ownership. This will force the creditor to provide concrete evidence of the debt, which they may not be able to do. If they fail to respond or provide incomplete information, you can **escalate the dispute** to a **611 dispute**, citing **15 U.S.C. § 1681s-2(a)(8)**. This will prompt the credit bureaus to **delete or suppress the account**, which can significantly improve your credit score.

It's crucial to remember that creditors often rely on intimidation and bullying tactics to get consumers to pay debts that may not be valid. **Don't let them push you around**. As a consumer, you have **rights under the FCRA and FDCPA** (Fair Debt Collection Practices Act) that protect you from unfair and deceptive practices. By knowing and asserting these rights, you can **take back control** of your credit and get the scores you deserve.

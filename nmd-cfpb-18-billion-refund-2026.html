They Promised to Fix Your Credit.
Then They Robbed You.

The CFPB just disbursed $1.8 billion to people scammed by predatory credit repair companies. Here's what they did, why it was illegal, and how you make sure it never happens to you again.

NMD ZAZA — The Credit Goat 🐐 · March 7, 2026 · Consumer Protection · 6 min read

$1,800,000,000.

That's how much the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau just returned to people who were scammed by fake credit repair companies. This isn't a lawsuit settlement that pays out $14 per person. This is $1.8 billion — real money — going back to real people who got robbed when they were already struggling.

Aye man. Let that number breathe for a second.

What Actually Happened

These companies positioned themselves as credit repair experts. They advertised on social media, ran ads promising 100-point score increases, and told desperate people exactly what they wanted to hear: "We can fix your credit."

Then they charged upfront fees. Collected money before doing a single thing. That's the move.

Here's the problem: that's a federal crime.

Under the Credit Repair Organizations Act (CROA) — a law that's been on the books since 1996 — credit repair companies are explicitly prohibited from charging consumers before services are fully performed. Not partially performed. Fully performed.

No setup fee. No deposit. No "processing charge." No exceptions. If they collected money first, they broke the law. Every single time.

🚨 Federal Violation

Charging upfront fees for credit repair services — before services are delivered — is a direct violation of 15 U.S.C. § 1679b under the Credit Repair Organizations Act. It doesn't matter how it's labeled. A "setup fee," "deposit," or "enrollment fee" collected before work is done is illegal.

The Scale of This

$1.8 billion means millions of victims. People with bad credit — already in a vulnerable spot — trusted these companies, handed over money, and got nothing meaningful in return.

Some of these operations ran for years. They scaled. They spent money on advertising because their margins were enormous. Take in $500 upfront from thousands of clients, deliver minimal service, keep most of it. It's a brutal hustle that preyed specifically on people who couldn't afford to lose that money.

The CFPB investigated, built cases, and went after them. This disbursement is the result.

"The best protection against a scam is knowing exactly what's legal — and what isn't. They count on you not knowing."

Your Rights Under Federal Law

Here's what every consumer needs to know cold. Under CROA, any legitimate credit repair company must:

  • 1
    Provide a written contract BEFORE any work begins — spelling out every service they'll perform and what it costs
  • 2
    Give you a 3-business-day right to cancel the contract with no penalty — no questions asked
  • 3
    Complete all services BEFORE charging you a single dollar
  • 4
    Tell you explicitly that you have the right to dispute your own credit report for free under the FCRA
  • 5
    Never advise you to create a "new" credit identity or misrepresent your SSN — that's federal fraud
💡 The Real Move

You can dispute your own credit report at no cost. Equifax, TransUnion, and Experian are legally required under the FCRA to investigate every dispute you submit — within 30 days. No middleman required. That's your right as a consumer.

The Warning Signs — Memorize These

A predatory credit repair company will:

✗ Charge you before doing any work
✗ Promise to remove accurate negative information
✗ Advise you to dispute everything — accurate or not
✗ Tell you to get a new credit identity or use a CPN (that's fraud)
✗ Pressure you to sign up immediately with a "limited time offer"
✗ Refuse to provide a written contract upfront

A legitimate operation will:

✓ Show you a written contract before collecting anything
✓ Explain your rights under FCRA and CROA
✓ Charge only after services are delivered
✓ Be transparent about what can and cannot be removed
✓ Never promise specific score increases

If You Were a Victim — Do This Now

  • 1
    File a complaint at CFPB.gov/complaint — This is how enforcement actions get started. Your complaint becomes part of the public record and fuels future investigations.
  • 2
    Check for disbursement eligibility — Visit CFPB.gov to see if you qualify for funds from this enforcement action.
  • 3
    Report to the FTC — File at ReportFraud.ftc.gov. FTC can pursue civil penalties and additional consumer remedies.
  • 4
    Contact your state AG — Most states have additional consumer protection laws that run parallel to CROA and FCRA. State-level enforcement can add teeth.
✅ Bottom Line

The CFPB returned $1.8 billion because people didn't know the law. Now you do. Upfront fees for credit repair = illegal. Always. Share this with anyone who's looking for help with their credit.

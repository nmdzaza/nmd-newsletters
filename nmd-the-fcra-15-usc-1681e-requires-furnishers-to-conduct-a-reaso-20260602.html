June 02, 2026

The Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA) is a powerful tool for consumers looking to build and maintain good credit. However, many furnishers of credit information fail to comply with the law, leaving individuals to deal with the consequences. **The FCRA requires furnishers to conduct a reasonable investigation of disputes**, but most don't, violating 15 U.S.C. § 1681s-2(b). This lack of investigation can lead to inaccurate information on your credit report, damaging your credit score and making it harder to get loans or credit at a reasonable interest rate.

When disputing errors on your credit report, it's essential to be specific and thorough. **Don't just state "not mine"** - this vague statement is unlikely to prompt a meaningful investigation. Instead, **specify the exact FCRA violation**, such as "**furnisher failed to conduct a reasonable investigation as required by 15 U.S.C. § 1681s-2(b)**". This approach shows that you're familiar with the law and willing to hold the furnisher accountable. Additionally, **demand the furnisher provide the original contract or application**, as required by 15 U.S.C. § 1681g(a)(1). If they can't produce this documentation, it's a strong indication that the debt is unverifiable and should be removed from your credit report.

Section 609 Of The Fcra Gives You The Right To Dispute Incomplete Or Inaccurate

June 22, 2026

When it comes to disputing errors on your credit report, understanding your rights under the Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA) is crucial. **Section 609 of the FCRA** gives you the right to dispute incomplete or inaccurate information on your report. However, what's equally important to know is that furnishers have a limited timeframe to investigate these disputes. Under **Section 1692g of the FDCPA**, they have **30-45 days** to verify the information. If they fail to do so, the item in question must be deleted from your report.

When disputing errors, it's essential to **cite specific FCRA sections** to assert your rights. For example, referencing **623(a)(1)(A)** for furnisher obligations can help establish the basis for your dispute. Using phrases like "**permissible purpose**" can also be effective in questioning the credit bureaus' investigation procedures. This approach demonstrates your understanding of the law and can prompt a more thorough review of your dispute.

The process of disputing errors involves several steps. First, you should send a **611 letter** to the Credit Reporting Agency (CRA), outlining the errors and requesting an investigation. Following this, you should submit a **604 dispute**, which formally initiates the dispute process. If the CRA fails to respond or verify the information, you can then send a **623(a)(2) letter** to the furnisher. This letter is a game-changer, as it directly challenges the furnisher's responsibility to verify the information and can lead to the removal of the disputed item if they fail to comply.

Understanding and navigating the dispute process can be complex, but **knowing your rights and the relevant laws** can significantly improve your chances of success.

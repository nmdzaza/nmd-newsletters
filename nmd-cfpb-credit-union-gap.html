Background

The CFPB Was
Your Last Line
of Defense.

The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau — the CFPB — was created after the 2008 financial crisis specifically to protect everyday consumers from banks, lenders, debt collectors, and credit bureaus. If a financial institution was abusing you, the CFPB was who you called.

It handled millions of complaints. It levied massive fines. It forced Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion to actually respond when you had a dispute. And now the Trump administration has declared its funding mechanism illegal — meaning the agency is on a path toward shutdown.

Who Handles Complaints

OCC / helpwithmybank.gov

Banks have a clear regulator. If you have a problem with Chase, BofA, or Wells Fargo — the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency handles it directly. There's a dedicated website, a clear process, and real enforcement power.

Covered

Nobody. That's the Problem.

The NCUA only handles complaints for credit unions with under $10 billion in assets. Anything above that was covered exclusively by the CFPB. If the CFPB goes away, members of large credit unions have no federal regulator to complain to.

Gap Exposed
Large Institution Example

JPMorgan Chase — $3.9T in Assets

Regulated by OCC. If Chase wrongly reports a debt on your credit file, you file with the OCC and they investigate. Process works with or without the CFPB.

OCC Has It

NFCU / PenFed — $40B+ in Assets

Both are above the NCUA's $10B threshold. A member who filed a complaint against PenFed with the NCUA was told it was outside their purview — and forwarded to the CFPB. If the CFPB is gone, that forward goes nowhere.

No Regulator
Credit Reporting Disputes

CFPB + OCC Both Watch Banks

If a bank furnisher reports something wrong on your credit file, you have two agencies watching that process.

Partially Covered

CFPB Was the Only Watchdog

Credit unions reporting errors on your file? That was exclusively CFPB territory for large CUs. No CFPB means no federal oversight of how big credit unions report to the bureaus.

How We Got Here
NMD

The CFPB's
Collapse
Timeline.

Feb 2025

Director Fired. Operations Halted.

Trump fired Director Rohit Chopra. Acting Director Russell Vought — Project 2025 architect — ordered all CFPB operations halted. Investigations, rulemaking, enforcement — all stopped. Probationary employees fired.

Full Shutdown Order
Nov 2025

DOJ Declares CFPB Funding Illegal

The DOJ's Office of Legal Counsel ruled the CFPB cannot draw from the Federal Reserve while the Fed operates at a loss. The agency anticipates exhausting all funds by early 2026.

Funding Declared Illegal
Dec 2025

Court Rules Against Vought — Temporarily

Judge Amy Berman Jackson ruled Vought cannot unilaterally refuse to request CFPB funding. $145 million was reluctantly requested — keeping the agency operational through March 2026.

Temporary Win
Feb 2026

DC Circuit Hears Arguments

The full U.S. Court of Appeals for the DC Circuit heard oral arguments on the future of the agency. The appellate judges will decide whether the CFPB survives in any meaningful form. Outcome still pending.

Legal Battle Ongoing
Now

Funded Through March 2026 — Then Unknown

The CFPB is technically still accepting complaints — but with up to 90% fewer staff, response times are gutted and enforcement is near zero. After March, nothing is guaranteed.

What This Means For You

What Happens
To You
Without the CFPB.

Credit Dispute Errors Go Unchallenged

The CFPB forced Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion to actually investigate disputes. Without that oversight, the bureaus have less pressure to fix errors — and more freedom to ignore them. Your dispute letters carry less weight.

Debt Collectors Get Bolder

The CFPB was the agency that enforced the Fair Debt Collection Practices Act. Without active enforcement, debt collectors who call at illegal hours, make false claims, or harass consumers face fewer consequences.

No Recourse with Large Credit Unions

If NFCU, PenFed, or any large credit union makes an error on your credit file, charges an illegal fee, or closes your account incorrectly — you now have no federal agency to escalate to. Your only option is a lawsuit.

State Patchwork Becomes Your Only Protection

Some states — like California and New York — have strong consumer protection laws. Others have almost nothing. Where you live now determines how much protection you have, which was never how federal consumer law was supposed to work.

Predatory Lending Ramps Back Up

The CFPB was actively watching payday lenders, credit card fee structures, and mortgage servicers. 22 enforcement cases were already dropped in 2025. Without a watchdog, the same practices that caused the 2008 crisis can resurface.

Your Complaint Database Disappears

The CFPB maintained a public database of consumer complaints — a powerful tool for identifying patterns of abuse. If the agency shuts down, that database and its institutional memory go with it.

TransUnion Just Slashed Your Mortgage Score Price — 40 Million Americans Could Now Qualify

TransUnion fired the latest shot in the credit score pricing war this week, cutting mortgage origination fees for VantageScore 4.0 to undercut FICO. If your lender switches, the score that's been blocking your mortgage approval may not even be used anymore.

Aye man, let me break this down for you because this is actually a big deal and most people in your position have no idea it's happening.

TransUnion just slashed the price it charges mortgage lenders to pull VantageScore 4.0. We're talking a direct shot at FICO's stranglehold on the mortgage market. And with Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac now accepting VantageScore 4.0 alongside FICO 10T, lenders have every financial incentive to make the switch.

Here's why that matters to you personally.

VantageScore 4.0 scores 40 million Americans that FICO can't score. Forty million. People with thin files, recent immigrants, folks rebuilding after a rough stretch. If you've ever been told "insufficient credit history" or watched a lender shrug at you, VantageScore 4.0 was built for exactly that situation.

It also uses trended data — meaning it looks at how your balances are moving over time, not just where they sit today. Paying down debt? VantageScore rewards that trajectory. FICO mostly sees a snapshot. VantageScore sees the story. That's a fundamentally different calculation, and for people actively rebuilding, it's a better one.

Now here's what you need to actually do with this information.

First, pull your VantageScore right now. Experian, Credit Karma, and most bank apps show you your VantageScore for free. Compare it to your FICO score. If your VantageScore is higher — and for a lot of rebuilders it is — you want to find a lender who's already using it.

Second, ask the right question when you shop lenders. Straight up ask: "Which scoring model do you use for mortgage origination — FICO 10T or VantageScore 4.0?" Lenders are now allowed to use either. The one they pick could be the difference between approved and denied at the same credit profile.

Third, understand what both models reward. Low utilization still matters on both. Payment history still matters on both. But VantageScore 4.0 specifically rewards positive momentum — trending down on balances, increasing on-time payments, adding positive accounts. If you're in the middle of a credit repair process, keep going. The new models will catch up to your progress faster than the old ones did.

Say man, this pricing war is not corporate drama. It's the mortgage industry being forced to reckon with the fact that FICO locked out a generation of creditworthy borrowers on arbitrary technicalities. TransUnion is betting billions that VantageScore 4.0 is the future of mortgage lending. Fannie and Freddie already agreed. The only thing left is for lenders to make the switch — and cheaper pricing just moved that timeline up.

If you've been sitting on the sidelines of homeownership because a FICO score said no — this is the year to start asking new questions. The model that said no might not even be the model your next lender uses.

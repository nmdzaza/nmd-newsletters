You'Re Being Ripped Off By Credit Bureaus If You Don'T Know That Under 15 U.S.C.

June 10, 2026

Are you being taken advantage of by credit bureaus? If you're not aware of your rights under the Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA), you might be. **The FCRA gives you the right to dispute any item on your credit report**, and the credit bureau is required to investigate. But here's the thing: **if they can't verify the debt within 30 days, it's supposed to be deleted**. Not just updated, deleted. This is stated clearly in 15 U.S.C. § 1681e(a), but credit bureaus often try to get around this by simply updating the information instead of removing it.

So, how can you use this to your advantage? **When disputing an item on your credit report, be specific**. Don't just send a generic letter stating that the information is incorrect. **Specify the exact Metro2 data fields that are incorrect**, such as the date of first delinquency or the current balance. This forces the credit bureau to investigate and verify the information. If they can't, **the item must be deleted**. This is a powerful tool for cleaning up errors on your credit report and improving your credit score.

But what if the credit bureau refuses to comply? **You have the right to take them to court**. Under 15 U.S.C. § 1681n, you can sue for actual damages and attorney fees if a credit bureau fails to comply with the FCRA. This is a strong incentive for credit bureaus to take your disputes seriously and investigate them thoroughly. By knowing your rights and being willing to assert them, you can take control of your credit report and ensure that it's accurate.

It can also give you access to better loan terms and higher credit limits.

They Borrowed $17.4 Million Against Homes
the Owners Didn't Know Were Collateral.

Eleven people were arrested in a coordinated FBI raid on a Hollywood mansion after running a years-long scheme to steal elderly Los Angeles homeowners' identities and take out high-interest loans against their properties — all without the owners knowing a single thing about it.

📅 March 25, 2026 ✍️ Za | NMD ZAZA ⏰ 6 min read

Who, what, when — the core facts.

On the morning of March 20, 2026, the FBI's Eurasian Organized Crime Task Force, working alongside IRS Criminal Investigation, executed simultaneous raids across the Los Angeles area. The centerpiece: a recently renovated Hollywood mansion with luxury vehicles parked out front. One suspect emerged from the front door in pajamas with his hands already raised before agents reached him.

Eleven people were taken into custody. Federal prosecutors allege they ran a coordinated mortgage fraud operation between January 2021 and May 2023 that targeted elderly homeowners across Hollywood, Hollywood Hills, Westwood, and Chinatown — neighborhoods where long-time residents often hold properties with significant equity, frequently owned free and clear.

The alleged scheme was methodical. The ring would identify elderly homeowners, steal their personal information and Social Security numbers, fabricate identification documents and email accounts in the victims' names, pull title reports, and then apply for high-interest "hard money" private real estate loans — using the victims' homes as collateral. The victims reportedly had no knowledge that their properties were being pledged. Total intended fraud: $17.4 million. Actual losses to lenders: approximately $6 million.

How the scheme worked — step by step.

Hard money loans are a specific category of real estate lending — high-interest, short-term loans made by private lenders rather than banks, typically collateralized by the property itself rather than the borrower's creditworthiness. They're used heavily in real estate investment when speed matters more than rate. They're also significantly easier to obtain with forged documentation than conventional bank loans.

According to prosecutors, the ring assembled a document forgery operation to support each fraudulent application. Submitted materials reportedly included fake bank statements, fabricated rental agreements, forged doctors' notes, and in some cases fabricated death certificates — the latter appearing to be used to make certain loan structures appear legitimate or to account for property status. The borrowers on paper were the elderly victims. The proceeds went to the defendants.

☠ How the Target Selection Worked

The ring specifically targeted high-equity properties in upscale LA neighborhoods where elderly long-term owners frequently hold homes free and clear. No mortgage means no mortgage lender monitoring the title. The victims were invisible to anyone who might have flagged unusual activity — until federal investigators started tracing the loan proceeds.

The operation ran for over two years before the FBI's Eurasian Organized Crime Task Force built a case sufficient for arrest warrants. The task force specializes in organized crime networks with ties to specific regional or ethnic criminal organizations operating in the Los Angeles area — a classification that shaped how investigators approached the case.

The eleven defendants — names and charges.

All eleven individuals face federal charges of conspiracy to commit wire fraud, wire fraud, aggravated identity theft, and money laundering. The defendants named in the case are:

The charges and what they carry.

Federal sentencing guidelines for the charges filed are severe. Wire fraud carries a maximum of 20 years per count. Conspiracy to commit wire fraud carries the same ceiling. Money laundering adds another 20 years per count on top. The most significant charge in terms of sentencing mechanics is aggravated identity theft — a charge that carries a mandatory two-year consecutive sentence under federal statute. That means regardless of what the judge decides on the fraud charges, those two years cannot be reduced, suspended, or run concurrently.

"The defendants stole the identities of elderly Los Angeles homeowners and fraudulently borrowed millions of dollars against the victims' properties — homes these victims didn't know were being used as collateral."

All eleven defendants are currently awaiting trial in federal court in the Central District of California. No trial dates have been publicly set as of the date of this article's publication.

What you should know — the facts that matter.

⚠ Key Takeaways from This Case

Hard money loans create a blind spot. Because hard money lending bypasses traditional bank underwriting — no DTI review, no income verification from the IRS, faster closings — the fraud had more surface area to operate. The lenders were private, the documentation requirements looser, and the processing faster.

Free-and-clear properties are a specific target class. Homes with no existing mortgage have no servicer monitoring title activity. When no monthly statement is going out, there is no institutional tripwire. The prosecution's case reportedly hinged on investigators following the loan proceeds — money that eventually flowed through shell accounts back to defendants.

Death certificates were allegedly forged. This is a specific escalation — creating fabricated death records to manipulate property records or loan structures is a federal crime on its own and suggests the ring had access to sophisticated document fabrication capability.

The mandatory consecutive sentence on aggravated identity theft changes the math. Unlike most fraud charges where sentences can run concurrently, the mandatory 2-year aggravated identity theft sentence stacks on top of whatever the court imposes for wire fraud and money laundering.

Updates and current status.

As of March 25, 2026, all eleven defendants have been arrested and are in federal custody or released on conditions pending trial. Arnold Moradians, who carried an outstanding immigration removal warrant, faces additional immigration proceedings concurrent with the criminal case.

The FBI's Eurasian Organized Crime Task Force has not publicly announced whether additional defendants are expected. Prosecutors have not yet indicated whether any of the eleven have entered into cooperation agreements.

The case is being prosecuted by the U.S. Attorney's Office for the Central District of California. The FBI and IRS Criminal Investigation are the lead investigative agencies.

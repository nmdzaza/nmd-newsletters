Building business credit is a crucial step for entrepreneurs looking to scale their operations. I've worked with hundreds of clients, and one thing is clear: having a solid business credit profile can make all the difference. Most gurus will tell you to start by establishing a business entity, but I'm here to give you the insider take. When setting up your entity, make sure to file in a state that offers strong business credit reporting, such as **Delaware or Nevada**. These states have a reputation for being business-friendly and offer a high level of anonymity, which can be beneficial for entrepreneurs who want to keep their personal and business finances separate.

For example, when filing in Delaware, you'll need to submit your certificate of formation to the Delaware Division of Corporations, along with a filing fee of $225. Once your entity is established, you can start building your business credit by applying for credit with vendors that report to the major credit bureaus. **Net 30 vendors like Uline, Grainger, or Quill** are great options to start with, as they can help you establish a strong credit foundation. These vendors offer credit terms that allow you to pay for goods or services within 30 days of receipt, and they report your payment history to the credit bureaus, which can help you build a positive credit profile.

One strategy that works is to use a **shelf corporation to expedite the business credit building process**. Aged shelf corporations can provide an instant credit profile, allowing you to access higher credit limits and better interest rates. For instance, a two-year-old shelf corporation can get approved for a $10,000 credit line with a vendor like Shell or Chevron. This can be a game-changer for entrepreneurs who need access to credit quickly.

Dark Side · Federal Crime · March 2026

They declared you dead on paper — then walked into the lender's office.

Eleven people were arrested across Los Angeles on March 19, 2026 for a $17.4 million fraud ring that forged death certificates on living elderly homeowners to drain their equity through private hard money lenders. The FBI's Eurasian Organized Crime Task Force called it one of the most sophisticated title fraud operations they had seen.

📅 March 25, 2026 ✍️ NMD ZAZA ⏰ 6 min read

The operation: steal a life on paper, drain equity in person.

The FBI was watching for years. On the morning of March 19, 2026, agents fanned out across Hollywood, North Hollywood, Glendale, and Thousand Oaks — eleven addresses, eleven arrests, before most of the defendants had poured their first coffee. The case: a multi-year conspiracy to steal the home equity of elderly Los Angeles property owners using some of the most elaborate fake documentation the bureau had encountered in a residential fraud case.

The operation had a name — Operation Hard Money — and a structure that borrowed from organized crime playbooks. Ringleaders Nazaret Chakrian, 65, of Hollywood, and Arnold Moradians, 57, also of Hollywood, allegedly built and directed a cell of nine co-conspirators who each played a role in the pipeline. They ran it clean for more than two years, from January 2021 through May 2023, accumulating $17.4 million in attempted fraudulent loans and confirming at least $6 million in actual losses before investigators closed the net.

The victims were elderly homeowners in some of LA's most affluent neighborhoods — Santa Monica, Hollywood Hills, Westwood, Chinatown, Hollywood. Many had significant equity. All of them were alive. None of them knew what was happening to their properties.

The document package: fake IDs, forged bank statements, and death certificates for the living.

The mechanics of the scheme were methodical. According to the federal indictment, the ring would first identify target properties — high-equity homes with elderly owners — and then acquire the owner's personal identifying information. Social Security numbers, dates of birth, property records. The foundation for everything that followed.

From there, the operation built what prosecutors describe as a full fraudulent document package: fake government-issued IDs, fabricated email accounts in the victims' names, forged bank statements, fake rental agreements, and false doctors' notes. Standard identity fraud equipment. But the element that made this operation different — the one that made federal investigators pause — was the death certificates.

The ring forged death certificates on living property owners. Not to hide that someone had died — to create the legal fiction that they had. Then they sent co-conspirators into meetings with private hard money lenders posing as heirs, estate representatives, or agents of the deceased owner's estate. The pitch: the estate needs to liquidate, we need a bridge loan against the property, here's the documentation. The lenders, operating faster and with less KYC scrutiny than traditional banks, issued the loans. The proceeds flowed into shell businesses and funnel accounts opened under additional stolen identities, then disappeared.

⚠ Why Hard Money Lenders?

Private hard money lenders were specifically targeted because they operate outside the standard banking KYC infrastructure. They move faster, ask fewer questions, and don't report through the same federal financial monitoring channels. The ring exploited the gap between speed and scrutiny that defines the hard money market.

Moradians and Sarkisian, both foreign nationals, also face potential deportation proceedings in addition to federal criminal exposure. Higdon's arrest in Naples, Florida indicates the ring had reach beyond Southern California — prosecutors allege he operated as an out-of-state co-conspirator who facilitated parts of the scheme remotely.

How the FBI broke it: IRS tracing, multi-agency coordination, years of work.

The investigation was run by the FBI's Eurasian Organized Crime Task Force — a unit that specializes in fraud networks with ethnic and regional structure, typically operating across organized cells rather than as loosely affiliated individuals. That framing shaped how investigators approached the case: not as a series of isolated fraudulent loan applications, but as a coordinated operation with hierarchy, roles, and a money movement architecture designed to obscure the trail.

"The growing problem of title fraud victimizes homeowners and lenders, many of whom are elderly."

— FBI Assistant Director Akil Davis

IRS Criminal Investigation was embedded in the task force throughout the investigation. Special Agent in Charge Tyler Hatcher described tracing "millions of dollars through a maze of fraudulent businesses and funnel accounts" — the classic money laundering architecture of shell LLCs and purpose-built bank accounts opened under stolen identities, used briefly, then abandoned. The paper trail was intentionally fragmented. IRS CI reassembled it.

Additional agencies on the task force included LAPD, the U.S. Postal Inspection Service, Los Angeles County Sheriff's Department, and Glendale Police Department. The coordination across city, county, state, and federal agencies — and across multiple geographic jurisdictions — suggests the investigation had been building for a significant period before the March 19 arrests.

The charges and what they carry.

The 15-count indictment includes charges of conspiracy to commit wire fraud, wire fraud, aggravated identity theft, and money laundering. The sentencing exposure is substantial:

  • Wire fraud: up to 20 years per count
  • Money laundering: up to 20 years per count
  • Aggravated identity theft: mandatory 2 years consecutive — stacked on top of any other sentence, no exceptions

The aggravated identity theft charge is the one federal prosecutors reach for when they want to guarantee prison time regardless of how other counts resolve. It cannot be reduced below the mandatory minimum, cannot be served concurrently with other sentences, and cannot be waived by a plea deal unless prosecutors agree to drop it. It is the federal charging tool designed specifically to ensure that identity theft defendants do real time.

All eleven defendants are presumed innocent until proven guilty. As of the date of publication, they have been charged and arraigned; no trial date had been publicly announced.

What you should know.

📝 Key Facts from the Indictment

Hard money lenders are a known soft target. The ring deliberately chose private lenders over banks because private lenders operate with less regulatory oversight and move faster. The same speed that makes hard money lending attractive to legitimate borrowers made it exploitable here.

📝 What the Prosecution Hinges On

The forged death certificates are the centerpiece. They transform what might have been a standard identity fraud case into something that carries emotional and legal weight. Prosecutors will lean on the fact that victims were declared dead on paper — without their knowledge — as evidence of premeditation and sophistication that supports the conspiracy and aggravated identity theft charges.

📝 What the Paper Trail Showed

IRS CI's role was decisive. The money laundering architecture — shell businesses, funnel accounts, layered transactions — was designed to obscure the trail. The IRS's ability to reassemble it across multiple entities is what made the case prosecutable. Financial crime cases live and die on the paper trail, and this one left one.

⚠ Title Fraud Is Rising Nationally

FBI Assistant Director Akil Davis specifically flagged title fraud as a growing problem in his statement, signaling that this case is part of a larger pattern — not an isolated incident. Homeowners, particularly elderly property owners with significant equity, are increasingly the targets of title and deed fraud operations that operate at scale.

Updates.

The FBI's Eurasian Organized Crime Task Force investigation is ongoing. Federal authorities have not publicly indicated whether additional arrests or charges are anticipated in connection with the same scheme. Both foreign nationals — Moradians (Iranian national) and Sarkisian (Azerbaijani national) — may face parallel immigration proceedings.

Legislative Alert — March 2026

Congress Just Dropped a Bill That Could Kill Scam Credit Repair Forever

A new federal bill says credit repair companies can't touch your money until your score actually goes up — and they have to prove it over 6 months. The scam industry just got put on notice.

Aye man — let me put you on something that hit the floor of Congress on March 20th and most people slept on it completely. A bill called the Ending Scam Credit Repair Act (ESCRA) was just introduced, and if it passes, it's gonna flip the entire credit repair industry upside down.

Here's what it does: no credit repair company can collect a single dollar from you until they've demonstrably improved your credit score — and they have to show that improvement over a 6-month period. Not promises. Not "disputes filed." Actual, documented, verified score improvement. No proof = no pay.

Why This Matters Right Now

The timing on this is not a coincidence. Literally this same month, the FTC sent out $10.9 million in refund checks to 443,048 consumers who got burned by Financial Education Services — a "credit repair" company that turned out to be a $213 million pyramid scheme. The FTC said they charged illegal upfront fees while delivering nothing.

443,048 victims. $213 million collected. Average refund check: $24.
That's the credit repair scam industry in one stat. You paid $99 a month. The government sent you $24 back. The ESCRA is Congress's response to exactly this pattern.

These companies have been running the same play for decades: charge $79–$199 a month, send generic dispute letters, collect for 6–12 months, then vanish or claim the bureaus were "unresponsive." Meanwhile you're out a thousand dollars and your credit is the same — or worse.

What the Bill Actually Says

Under the ESCRA, credit repair organizations would be:

Banned from collecting any payment until 6 months after services begin. And they only get paid if there's a verifiable, documented improvement to your credit score — not just disputes filed, but real results reflected on your report.

Required to provide transparent, itemized proof of every action taken and every result achieved. No vague "we submitted disputes" language. They have to show their work.

Held strictly liable for violations. Consumers can sue directly, and the FTC gets enhanced enforcement authority. This isn't a slap-on-the-wrist situation.

How to Protect Yourself Right Now — Before It Passes

Say man, the bill hasn't passed yet. That means scam companies are still out here operating the same way TODAY. Here's what you do right now:

Rule #1: Never pay a monthly fee to a credit repair company. That model is the scam. Real credit help is either free knowledge you apply yourself, or a one-time service fee for specific work done.

Rule #2: Under the existing Credit Repair Organizations Act (CROA), charging upfront fees before services are performed is already illegal. If a company is doing that, you can dispute the charges AND report them to the FTC at reportfraud.ftc.gov.

Rule #3: Dispute letters are not magic. A blank dispute letter does nothing. What works is targeted disputes tied to specific errors, specific furnishers, and specific FCRA violations. That's the difference between noise and results.

The ESCRA's core logic: If a company is confident they can fix your credit, they should be willing to get paid only after they do. If they demand payment before delivering results — that's the only answer you need about whether they're legit.

What This Means for You Going Forward

If you've been victimized by FES or a similar company between May 2019 and May 2022 specifically, check ftc.gov/refunds — you may have a check waiting. The FTC mailed 443,000 of them.

If you're actively looking for credit help right now — here's the real talk. You don't need a monthly subscription. You need a clear look at your reports, targeted disputes on the items that are hurting your score, and a game plan. That's it. No $99/month. No empty promises.

The ESCRA is moving through Congress. Watch it. If it passes, the entire industry that's been eating your money will either adapt or die. Either way — your best move is to stop waiting on legislation and handle your credit yourself with the right tools, today.

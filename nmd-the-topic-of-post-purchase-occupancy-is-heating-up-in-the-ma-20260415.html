For example, a buyer offering $640,000 on a $635,000 listed house may be expected to concede on post-purchase occupancy, which could add up to $5,000 in extra costs, assuming a $5,000 per month mortgage payment.

In the context of **fractional real estate investing**, this trend highlights the importance of considering **all the costs and concessions** involved in a deal. By applying such a framework, investors can better navigate the complexities of the market and avoid **costly mistakes**. For instance, a buyer using this framework may decide to **walk away from a deal** that demands excessive concessions, or **negotiate a better price** to offset the costs.

So, what's the most critical factor to consider when evaluating a real estate investment opportunity? While a **lower purchase price** may be attractive, it's essential to balance this with the potential **risks and concessions** involved. **More favorable terms**, such as a longer closing period or more assistance with closing costs, can also be beneficial.

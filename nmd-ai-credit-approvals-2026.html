AI & Credit · March 2026

AI is now deciding
your approvals.

Lenders are replacing FICO with AI models that read rent payments, utilities, and spending behavior. Colorado just passed the first law forcing them to explain how. Here's exactly who wins, who gets left behind, and what you can do right now.

📅 March 11, 2026 ✍️ NMD ZAZA ⏱ 6 min read

The scorecard just changed — and most people don't know it

For decades, the game was simple: your three-digit FICO score was the gatekeeper. Banks, landlords, car dealers — everyone pulled that number and made their call. You knew the rules. You played the game accordingly.

That game is over. In 2026, lenders are deploying AI scoring models that pull alternative data sources — on-time rent payments, utility bills, subscription payments, even how you move money between accounts — and layering that on top of your traditional credit file. The AI doesn't care that you have a 700. It cares whether that 700 is backed by behavior it can trust.

📊 The Numbers Are Real

Banks and fintechs using AI credit scoring are reporting up to a 35% increase in automated approval rates and a 20–50 basis point compression in quoted loan spreads. More people getting approved at better rates — but only the people the AI can read clearly.

Commonwealth Credit Union just partnered with Zest AI to launch the CU Lending Collective — bringing enterprise-grade AI scoring to small credit unions across the country. Auto loans, personal loans, credit cards: all running through AI models that look way beyond your traditional score. This isn't a big-bank experiment anymore. It's hitting Main Street.

Colorado just made lenders explain their AI — and that changes everything

Here's the move that most credit content is sleeping on: Colorado's SB 24-205 went into effect in February 2026. It requires any financial institution operating in Colorado that uses AI to make or influence credit decisions to disclose how that AI works — what data it used, what factors it weighted, and why you were approved or denied.

This is the first state law of its kind in the U.S. And it's already being watched closely by consumer advocates and regulators in 12 other states drafting similar legislation.

⚖️ Why This Law Matters for You

Before this law, if an AI denied your loan, the lender just said "insufficient credit history" and closed the door. Now in Colorado — and soon in more states — you can demand to know exactly which data points caused the denial. That's leverage. That's a paper trail. That's a dispute opportunity.

The EU went even further. The EU's CCD2 directive and AI Act, now fully in force, require all lenders operating in Europe to prove their AI scoring models are fair, explainable, and free from discriminatory patterns. U.S. regulators are watching the EU framework closely. Explainability requirements are coming nationwide — it's just a matter of when.

What AI actually looks at when it pulls your file

Here's where the rubber meets the road. AI scoring models don't just read your FICO number — they analyze a full behavioral profile built from every data point they can access. Understanding what they're reading is the first step to optimizing for it.

"In an AI-driven credit world, the consumer with on-time rent payments, consistent utility bills, and a clean dispute history beats the consumer with a high FICO and nothing else behind it."

The old playbook was: hit 700, get approved. The new playbook is: build a full behavioral credit footprint that an AI model can read, trust, and reward. That's a different game — and it favors the people who know the rules.

Who wins and who gets wrecked in AI credit scoring

Let's be direct. AI scoring is not neutral. It has winners and losers — and the split is clearer than ever.

  • ✓
    Winners: People who pay everything on time. Rent, utilities, subscriptions — all of it showing up in alternative data now. If you pay on time across all your bills, AI models have a rich positive signal to work with. Traditional FICO would have ignored most of this. AI doesn't.
  • ✓
    Winners: People who dispute errors aggressively. AI models pull all three bureaus. One inaccurate derogatory on Equifax that you let sit there quietly used to be survivable. Now it's a flag across every AI system that reads your file. Clean bureaus = clean AI reads.
  • ✗
    Losers: People with thin files. If you have fewer than 3–4 active tradelines with consistent history, AI models default to conservative decisions. Not because you're bad credit — because they don't have enough data. You're invisible to them, and invisible gets denied.
  • ✗
    Losers: People who paid cash for everything. The "I don't believe in credit" crowd is about to feel this hard. Alternative data is the saving grace — but you have to actively report your rent, utilities, and bills to make it work for you.
  • ✗
    Losers: People with stale, unresolved disputes. An error on your credit report that you filed once and forgot about is poison in an AI system. It doesn't resolve itself. It flags every read. You need a live dispute strategy, not a one-time letter from 2023.

The 5 moves to make right now

This is not theoretical. These are the exact steps that position you to win in the AI scoring environment that's already live across thousands of lenders.

  • 1
    Report your rent to all three bureaus. Use Experian RentBureau, Rental Kharma, or LevelCredit to get 12–24 months of on-time rent reporting added to your file. This is free or low-cost alternative data that AI models reward heavily.
  • 2
    Dispute every inaccuracy across all three bureaus now. Not just Experian. Not just the big one. All three. AI systems read all three and aggregate the signal. One bureau with a clean dispute resolution improves every AI read of your file.
  • 3
    Add at least 2 new tradelines this year. Secured cards, credit-builder loans, or authorized user tradelines with age behind them. AI scoring models need depth of data. Give them something to work with.
  • 4
    Monitor your trended data direction. Pull your FICO 10T score if your lender offers it. Check whether your utilization trend is moving down. A rising balance trend is a red flag to AI — even if your current utilization is fine.
  • 5
    If you're in Colorado — demand disclosure on any AI denial. SB 24-205 gives you the legal right to know what data the AI used and how it weighted your decision. Use it. More states are coming. Know your rights before they spread.

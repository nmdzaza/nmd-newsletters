The Credit Bureaus Are Not Your Friends - They'Re Obligated To Conduct A "Reason

May 30, 2026

The credit bureaus are not your friends. Their primary goal is to collect and sell your data, not to ensure the accuracy of your credit report. When you dispute an item on your report, they are obligated to conduct a **"reasonable investigation"** under 15 USC 1681e(a), but they often don't. This lack of effort can lead to incorrect information remaining on your report, damaging your credit score and ultimately affecting your financial opportunities.

When disputing an item, it's essential to **cite specific FCRA violations** like 15 USC 1681s-2(b) and provide **detailed evidence** to support your claim. This can include proof of payment, identification of incorrect information, or any other relevant documentation. By doing so, you're holding the credit bureaus accountable for their actions and increasing the likelihood of a successful dispute. Additionally, **demand that the bureaus verify the debt** under 15 USC 1692g and require the furnisher to **certify the debt is valid** under 15 USC 1681s-2(a)(3). This is a crucial step in getting items deleted from your report.

If the credit bureaus don't comply with your dispute, don't back down. **File a complaint with the CFPB**, and be prepared to provide evidence to support your claim. The law is on your side, and you have the right to accurate and fair credit reporting. By taking control of your credit and disputing inaccurate information, you can improve your credit score and increase your access to better financial opportunities.

$68 Million Wasn't Enough.
They targeted your community.

Colony Ridge charged Hispanic families 12% interest, skipped affordability checks, sold land without water or power — then let them default. The DOJ just settled for $68M. But the credit destruction? That's on you to fix.

📅 March 11, 2026 ✍️ NMD ZAZA ⏱ 6 min read

This is what predatory lending looks like in 2026.

A family sees an ad in Spanish. The imagery is warm, familiar — cultural touchstones, a promise of land ownership, financial independence. They sign. They move in. Then the bills come.

The interest rate? 10.9% to 12.9% — at a time when market rates sat between 2.35% and 4.05%. Nobody verified whether they could afford the payments. Nobody told them the land lacked running water, sewer hookups, or electricity. When they couldn't pay, they defaulted. When they defaulted, their credit collapsed.

This wasn't a fringe operation. Colony Ridge sold over 12,000 land installment contracts in Liberty County, northeast of Houston, Texas. They built an entire marketing machine targeting Spanish-speaking communities — nearly exclusively Spanish-language social media ads, culturally specific imagery, the works. And the result was predictable: mass defaults, mass credit destruction, and the federal government eventually showing up three years too late.

⚠ DOJ Settlement — February 10, 2026

The Department of Justice and the State of Texas settled with Colony Ridge for $68 million. The violations: Equal Credit Opportunity Act (ECOA) and the Fair Housing Act (FHA). Charges included failure to verify ability to repay, predatory interest rates targeting protected classes, and deceptive marketing practices.

Here's the part that stings: of that $68 million settlement, $48 million goes to infrastructure improvements — roads, water, sewer, drainage. And $20 million goes to law enforcement presence in the subdivisions. Not a single dollar is earmarked as direct restitution to the borrowers who were harmed.

The people whose credit scores were shredded by mass defaults and foreclosures? They're responsible for cleaning that up themselves.

How predatory loans destroy your credit — and why it's not your fault

When a lender sells you a loan they know you can't afford, they are engineering your default. They get the land back. They get to resell it. And you're left with a trail of negative marks that can follow you for seven years:

Credit Event Score Impact Duration on Report
Missed payment (30+ days late) -60 to -110 pts 7 years from date of delinquency
Foreclosure filing -85 to -160 pts 7 years from filing date
Deficiency judgment (after sale) -50 to -100 pts 7 years from judgment
Collection account (unpaid balance) -50 to -110 pts 7 years from original delinquency
Inquiry storm (multiple applications) -5 to -15 pts each 2 years from inquiry date

"They count on you not knowing your rights. They count on you thinking the debt is legitimate because you signed something. A signature on a fraudulent or illegal loan agreement does not obligate you to destroy your credit quietly."

What the law actually says — your rights under ECOA and the FHA

The Equal Credit Opportunity Act makes it illegal to discriminate in credit transactions based on race, national origin, sex, marital status, age, or religion. Targeting a specific ethnic group with inferior loan terms is an ECOA violation. Full stop.

The Fair Housing Act extends those protections to housing-related transactions — including the type of land installment contracts Colony Ridge was selling. Marketing exclusively to one ethnic community with products you know are likely to result in default isn't strategy. It's illegal discrimination.

Here's what that means for you if you were a Colony Ridge borrower — or anyone who's been in a similar predatory lending situation:

  • 1 The negative items may be disputable. If your defaults and foreclosures trace back to a loan that was originated in violation of federal law — including ECOA violations — you have grounds to challenge those marks. Gather every document: the contract, the marketing materials, the payment history.
  • 2 File a complaint with the DOJ and HUD. If you were a Colony Ridge borrower, contact the DOJ Civil Rights Division and HUD to document your individual experience. This builds the record. Even if the settlement is closed, your individual case may still have merit.
  • 3 Consult an ECOA/FHA attorney — on contingency. ECOA allows for recovery of actual damages, punitive damages up to $10,000, and attorney's fees. Attorneys who specialize in lending discrimination take cases on contingency because the law makes defendants pay when they lose.
  • 4 Dispute every connected negative mark. Missed payments, foreclosure notices, collection accounts — anything connected to the predatory loan should be disputed with all three bureaus, citing the underlying lending violation. Be specific: reference the DOJ settlement, the ECOA violation, and your right to accurate, complete, and fairly obtained credit information.
  • 5 Rebuild while you fight. The dispute process takes time. In parallel, build new positive credit history. Secured cards, credit-builder loans, authorized user tradelines — these compound your score while the negative marks are challenged.

How to spot the next Colony Ridge before you sign

Predatory lenders don't announce themselves. They use beautiful marketing. They target communities that have historically been denied credit access — making the offer feel like opportunity rather than a trap. Here are the non-negotiable red flags:

💡 Red Flag Checklist

Rate more than 2x the market rate — always compare at least 3 lenders. No income verification — any legitimate lender verifies your ability to repay. Marketing in only one language to only one ethnic group — this is targeted predatory behavior, not cultural competency. Land or property sold "as-is" without infrastructure — no water, power, or sewer access means you're buying a problem. Pressure to sign fast — legitimate deals don't evaporate if you take 48 hours to review.

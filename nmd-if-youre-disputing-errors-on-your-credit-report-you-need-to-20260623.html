If You'Re Disputing Errors On Your Credit Report, You Need To Know That Section

June 23, 2026

When it comes to disputing errors on your credit report, knowledge is power. The Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA) is a federal law that protects consumers from inaccurate or incomplete information on their credit reports. **Section 609 of the FCRA** requires furnishers to conduct a reasonable investigation of your dispute, and **Section 623** makes them liable for damages if they fail to do so. This means that if you're disputing an error on your credit report, the furnisher must take your dispute seriously and investigate the matter thoroughly.

To effectively dispute an error on your credit report, you need to be specific and detailed in your dispute letter. **Include a specific reference to the Metro2 data fields that are inaccurate**, such as the Date of First Delinquency or the Current Balance. For example, "I dispute the collection account from XYZ Credit Corp, as the Date of First Delinquency is listed as 02/2020, but my records show the last payment was made in 01/2020. I request that you provide a copy of the original contract and payment history to support this claim." This level of detail shows that you've done your research and are serious about resolving the dispute.

When sending your dispute, **use certified mail to create a paper trail**. This will provide you with proof of mailing and receipt, which can be useful if you need to escalate the dispute. Be sure to **keep a record of all correspondence**, including dates, times, and details of conversations. This will help you track the progress of your dispute and ensure that the furnisher is taking your dispute seriously.

If the furnisher fails to respond or investigate your dispute, **you may be entitled to damages under Section 616 of the FCRA**. This can include actual damages, such as financial losses, as well as statutory damages, which can be up to $1,000. Don't let inaccurate information hold you back from achieving your financial goals. Take control of your credit report and demand that furnishers provide accurate information.

Disputing errors on your credit report can be a complex and time-consuming process, but it's worth the effort. By knowing your rights under the FCRA and taking a proactive approach to disputing errors, you can **improve your credit score and achieve financial stability**.

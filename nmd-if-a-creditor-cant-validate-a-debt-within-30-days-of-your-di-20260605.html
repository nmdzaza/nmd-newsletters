If A Creditor Can'T Validate A Debt Within 30 Days Of Your Dispute Under 15 Usc

June 05, 2026

When dealing with debt collectors, it's essential to know your rights. One crucial piece of legislation is 15 USC 1692g, which states that if a creditor can't validate a debt within 30 days of your dispute, they must cease collection and delete the tradeline. This is a powerful tool in your arsenal, and using it effectively can be a game-changer for your credit score.

To utilize this law, you need to craft a dispute letter that leaves no room for misinterpretation. Here's a phrase you can use: "Pursuant to 15 USC 1681i(a)(1), I dispute this account as inaccurate, please provide the original contract and proof of ownership, if no response is received within 30 days, I expect deletion under 15 USC 1692g". Sending this exact phrase to creditors sets a clear expectation and puts them on notice. If they fail to respond or validate the debt, you can follow up and demand deletion.

After sending the dispute letter, it's crucial to follow up with the credit bureaus. You can use 15 USC 1681s-2(b) to support your claim, which requires the bureaus to investigate and correct any inaccuracies. By citing this law, you're holding the bureaus accountable and ensuring they take your dispute seriously. This two-pronged approach – disputing with the creditor and following up with the bureaus – can lead to significant improvements in your credit score.

Deleting incorrect tradelines can boost your credit score by 50-100 points or more, depending on the severity of the error. This can be the difference between getting approved for a loan or credit card and being rejected. By taking control of your credit report and using the laws to your advantage, you can unlock better financial opportunities and build a stronger credit foundation.
